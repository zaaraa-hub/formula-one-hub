How F1 Cars Work

🏎️

Aerodynamics

F1 cars generate massive downforce using front and rear wings, allowing them to corner at speeds impossible for regular cars. The floor and diffuser create ground effect, pushing the car into the track.

⚙️

Power Unit

The 2026 power unit combines a 1.6L V6 turbo engine with a much more powerful electric motor, increasing electrical power while delivering over 1000 bhp. The new regulations triple the electrical power compared to previous seasons.

🔄

Active Aerodynamics

F1 cars in 2026 no longer use traditional DRS. Instead, they use active front and rear wings that change between high-downforce mode for corners and low-drag mode on straights. An electrical boost system also helps drivers during overtaking.

🛞

Tyres

Pirelli supplies 5 compounds from C1 (hardest) to C5 (softest). Softer tyres give more grip but wear faster, making tyre strategy crucial.

🛑

Braking

Carbon fibre brakes can slow the car from 300 km/h to 80 km/h in under 2 seconds. Brake temperatures exceed 1000°C during heavy braking zones like Monza and Baku.

🪶

Weight & Materials

F1 cars weigh around 768kg including the driver under the 2026 regulations. They're built from carbon fibre composites, making them incredibly strong yet lightweight. The chassis can survive massive impacts safely.
