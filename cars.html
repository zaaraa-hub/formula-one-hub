How F1 Cars Work

🏎️

Aerodynamics

F1 cars generate massive downforce using front and rear wings, allowing them to corner at speeds impossible for regular cars. The floor and diffuser create ground effect, pushing the car into the track.

⚙️

Power Unit

The 2026 power unit produces over 1000 bhp combining a 1.6L V6 turbo engine with a powerful electric motor. The new regulations triple the electrical power compared to previous seasons.

🔄

DRS System

Drag Reduction System opens the rear wing flap to reduce drag on straights, giving drivers up to 20 km/h extra speed when within 1 second of the car ahead.

🛞

Tyres

Pirelli supplies 5 compounds from C1 (hardest) to C5 (softest). Softer tyres give more grip but wear faster. Teams must use at least 2 compounds per race, making strategy crucial.

🛑

Braking

Carbon fibre brakes can slow the car from 300 km/h to 80 km/h in under 2 seconds. Brake temperatures exceed 1000°C during heavy braking zones like Monza and Baku.

🪶

Weight & Materials

F1 cars weigh just 798kg including the driver. They're built from carbon fibre composites, making them incredibly strong yet lightweight. The chassis can survive massive impacts safely.
